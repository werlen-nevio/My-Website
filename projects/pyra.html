Case study

PYRA Webinterface

A web dashboard for digitalizing fire safety inspections, managing buildings, users, templates, and reports for Swiss fire departments and municipalities.

The problem

Fire safety inspections in Switzerland are still mostly paper-based. Inspectors carry checklists into buildings, fill them in by hand, then re-type the data into legacy systems back at the office. Reports get lost, photos never make it to the right place, and follow-ups slip through the cracks.

PYRA replaces that paper trail with a single web app that fire departments and municipalities can use to plan inspections, capture findings on-site, and generate the official reports automatically.

What I built

I worked on the web interface — the part inspectors and admins see in the browser. The goal was to make a tool that feels fast and obvious to people who do not think of themselves as "computer people."

  • Building & unit management with hierarchical navigation
  • Editable inspection templates that drive the on-site checklists
  • User and role management for departments and municipalities
  • Report generation with print-ready output
Every screen is one decision away from the action the user actually came here to do.

Design notes

Tables that don't get in the way

Most of the app is tables — buildings, inspections, users, reports. Instead of fighting that, I leaned into it: dense rows, sticky headers, inline status, and a single primary action per row. No modal stacks.

Templates as first-class data

Inspection templates are the core of the system. Editing one had to feel safer than it looks. Versioning, draft vs. published states, and a preview of the rendered checklist were table stakes.

What I learned

Building for users who are not developers forces every shortcut to justify itself. A keyboard shortcut nobody finds is not a shortcut. A toast that disappears in 3 seconds is a bug. Most of the wins on this project came from removing things, not adding them.
